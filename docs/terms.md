# Terms of the public hub

A draft, written by engineers for a lawyer to check; not legal advice. Everything in square brackets is to be
filled in. These terms are between the hub's operator and a community that keeps its node on the public hub at
okay-mutual.okay-effects.workers.dev. A member's relation is with the community, under the community's own
rules; the app and the software are open source and come with their own licence.

## 1. What the hub is

1.1. The hub is a machine on which communities keep their nodes of Okay Mutual, open-source software for mutual
credit. It is run by [the operator's name, address and e-mail], "the operator".

1.2. The operator hosts. The operator is not a party to the members' deals, does not issue, hold, sell, buy or
exchange units, holds no member's money, and has no key by which a community or a member signs.

1.3. The hub is offered [free of charge]. Fees, if any, are announced [60 days] ahead and are paid in money, never
in units.

## 2. Creating a community

2.1. Whoever creates a community here accepts these terms and the [processing agreement](processing.md) on the
community's behalf, and confirms they may do so.

2.2. The community is responsible for its members, its rules, its policy, its decisions, and that all of them are
lawful — among others the tax rules on its members' deals and data-protection law as the controller of its
members' data.

2.3. The community keeps at least two managers (the app asks for it) and keeps copies of itself (*Community → Move
or back up*).

## 3. What is not allowed

3.1. Selling units for money, buying them, exchanging them back, quoting a price for them, or offering them as an
investment.

3.2. Using the hub for anything unlawful: fraud, laundering money, evading tax, trading in what may not be traded.

3.3. Keeping in names, memos, board entries or rules data beyond what a community's account book needs — in
particular health, religion, political views or other special categories of personal data.

3.4. Attacking the hub or other communities, or loading it beyond the limits it states.

## 4. What the operator may do

4.1. Suspend a community that breaks these terms or the law, after a notice to its managers where the law and
safety allow, and end its hosting if it does not stop.

4.2. Refuse to host a community, limit how many communities an address creates.

4.3. Stop the hub, with [60 days] notice to every community, during which each takes its file.

## 5. The way out

A community may leave at any time: it saves its file and brings it to another hub or its own node; the operator
deletes it from the hub within [30 days] after it closes its place here.

## 6. No warranty; liability

6.1. The software comes as it is, under its licence (Apache 2.0), with no warranty. The operator does its best
to keep the hub running and the communities' data safe, keeps backups, and gives no guarantee of availability.

6.2. The operator is liable as far as the law makes it liable and no further: not for the members' deals, not for
a loss of units (units are not money and have no price), not for what a community decides.

## 7. Changes

A change of these terms is published [30 days] before it applies; a community that does not accept it may leave
(point 5).

## 8. Law

Polish law applies; disputes go to the courts of [the operator's seat], without taking from a consumer any right
the law of their country gives.
