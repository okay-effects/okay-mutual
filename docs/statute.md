# The foundation's statute — a draft

A draft for a lawyer to rewrite, not a document to sign. It follows the usual shape of a Polish foundation's
statute (the Act on Foundations of 6 April 1984); the [Polish text](pl/statute.md) is the working one, the others
are translations for reading. Everything in square brackets is to be decided. Why it is built this way is in
[who owns what](organisation.md). Not legal advice.

## Chapter 1. General provisions

**§ 1.** 1. The foundation named "[Okay Mutual Foundation]", hereinafter "the Foundation", is established by
[the founder's name], hereinafter "the Founder", by a notarial deed drawn up on [date] before [notary] in [place].

2. The Foundation acts under the Act on Foundations of 6 April 1984 and this statute.

**§ 2.** 1. The Foundation's seat is [city]. 2. It acts in the Republic of Poland and, as far as its purposes
require, abroad. 3. It is established for an indefinite time. 4. It has legal personality. 5. The minister
competent for its purposes is the minister competent for [the economy / digital affairs — to be confirmed].

**§ 3.** The Foundation may use its name in other languages, a seal and a mark of its own.

## Chapter 2. Purposes and how they are pursued

**§ 4.** The Foundation's purposes are:

1. to develop and keep freely available the open software, the protocol and the documentation by which
   communities keep mutual credit accounts among their members;
2. to support local economies, entrepreneurship and employment by helping communities of businesses and people
   exchange goods and services among themselves;
3. to spread knowledge of mutual credit, of community economies and of the safe use of digital tools;
4. to protect the name and the integrity of the project for the benefit of everyone who uses it.

**§ 5.** The Foundation pursues its purposes by:

1. publishing the software under an open licence and maintaining it;
2. operating publicly available infrastructure on which communities may keep their accounts, and helping them
   run their own;
3. preparing and publishing documentation, model rules and model agreements, and commissioning legal and security
   reviews whose results are published;
4. training, advice and events;
5. translation of the software and the documents;
6. cooperation with communities, public bodies, scientific institutions and other organisations.

**§ 6. What the Foundation does not do.** In pursuing its purposes the Foundation:

1. does not issue, sell, buy or exchange the units in which communities keep their accounts, and takes no fee in
   them;
2. does not accept, hold or transfer money or other assets of the members of communities;
3. does not admit or exclude a community's members, set their limits, or decide a community's rules or policy;
4. does not hold the keys by which a community or its members sign;
5. may not make the use of the open software depend on using its infrastructure.

A resolution contrary to this paragraph is void.

## Chapter 3. Assets and income

**§ 7.** 1. The Foundation's assets are the founding fund of [amount] złoty contributed by the Founder, and the
assets it acquires later. 2. [Of the founding fund, [amount, at least 1,000] złoty is assigned to business
activity — only if § 9 is kept.]

**§ 8.** The Foundation's income comes from: 1) donations, inheritances and bequests; 2) grants and subsidies;
3) fees for its paid statutory activity; 4) income from its assets; 5) [income from business activity — only if
§ 9 is kept].

**§ 9.** [1. The Foundation may carry on business activity, only in the extent serving its statutory purposes,
in the field of: hosting and related services; software services; training. 2. All income from it serves the
statutory purposes.] *— to be kept or removed on the lawyer's advice: whether hosting for a fee is a paid
statutory activity or a business activity.*

**§ 10.** 1. The whole income of the Foundation serves its statutory purposes. 2. The Foundation does not
distribute profit to the Founder, the members of its bodies or its employees.

**§ 11.** The Foundation may not:

1. grant loans or secure obligations with its assets in favour of the Founder, the members of its bodies, its
   employees or persons close to them;
2. transfer its assets to them on terms other than those applied to third parties, in particular free of charge
   or on preferential terms;
3. use its assets for them on terms other than those applied to third parties, unless this follows directly from
   a statutory purpose;
4. buy goods or services from entities in which they take part on terms other than those applied to third
   parties or at prices higher than market prices.

## Chapter 4. The Foundation's bodies

**§ 12.** The Foundation's bodies are: 1) the Board; 2) the Council.

### The Board

**§ 13.** 1. The Board has from one to [three] members, appointed for [three] years by the Founder, and after the
Council is first complete — by the Council on the Founder's proposal. 2. A member may be appointed again.
3. Membership ends by resignation, death, the end of the term or dismissal by the body that appoints.

**§ 14.** 1. The Board directs the Foundation's work and represents it. 2. It decides everything not reserved
for the Council. 3. Where the Board has more than one member, declarations of will in matters above [amount]
złoty are made by two members acting jointly; otherwise by one.

**§ 15.** A member of the Board may be paid for the work, in an amount set by the Council.

### The Council

**§ 16.** 1. The Council is the Foundation's body of supervision and of the communities' voice. 2. It has from
three to [nine] members. 3. Its first members are appointed by the Founder. 4. Each community that has used the
Foundation's infrastructure for at least [six] months under an agreement and has at least [ten] members may name
one member of the Council; when there are more such communities than seats for them, they choose among
themselves in the way the Council's rules set. 5. At least [half] of the Council's seats are for members named by
communities once at least [three] communities are entitled to name them. 6. Members of the Council are not
members of the Board, are not subordinate to them and are not their close relatives. 7. Members of the Council
are not paid, apart from the return of justified costs.

**§ 17.** The Council:

1. appoints and dismisses members of the Board, as § 13 says;
2. approves the yearly plan and budget, and the yearly report and accounts;
3. sets the Foundation's fees for communities;
4. approves, before it is made, every contract between the Foundation and the Founder, a member of a body, an
   employee, a person close to them, or an entity in which any of them takes part — the person concerned not
   voting;
5. supervises that § 6 is kept;
6. decides on changes of the statute, a merger and liquidation, as chapters 5 and 6 say.

**§ 18.** 1. The Council decides by a simple majority with at least half its members present, unless the statute
says otherwise. 2. It meets at least twice a year, and may meet and vote by electronic means.

### Openness

**§ 19.** The Foundation publishes on its website: 1) this statute and the rules of its bodies; 2) its yearly
report and accounts; 3) its fees; 4) every contract approved under § 17 point 4, with its subject and price;
5) the results of the legal and security reviews it commissions.

## Chapter 5. Changes of the statute, merger

**§ 20.** 1. The statute is changed by the Council by a majority of two thirds, with the Founder's consent while
the Founder is alive [and takes part in the Foundation]. 2. § 6, § 10, § 11, § 19 and § 22 may be changed only
unanimously, and never so as to let the Foundation do what § 6 forbids.

**§ 21.** The Foundation may merge with another foundation of similar purposes by the Council's decision taken by
a majority of two thirds; never if the purposes would change in substance.

## Chapter 6. Liquidation

**§ 22.** 1. The Foundation is liquidated when its purposes are achieved or its means are exhausted, by the
Council's decision taken by a majority of two thirds. 2. The liquidator is the Board unless the Council appoints
another. 3. What remains after liquidation goes to a non-profit organisation of similar purposes named by the
Council — in the first place to an association of the communities that used the Foundation's infrastructure,
when there is one. 4. In no case do the name, the marks, the domain names or the infrastructure pass to the
Founder, a member of a body or a person or entity related to them. 5. The software stays under its open licence.

## Chapter 7. Final provisions

**§ 23.** The Foundation submits its yearly report to the competent minister and publishes it.

**§ 24.** This statute enters into force on the day the Foundation is entered in the National Court Register.

## Notes for the lawyer

- **§ 6** is the heart of it: it turns [the software's properties](legal.md) into the organisation's own limits,
  so that a later board cannot make the Foundation an issuer, a holder of funds or a governor of communities.
- **§ 9** depends on the answer to the first open question: paid statutory activity or business activity.
- **§ 11, § 16 point 6, § 17 point 4, § 19** are written with a later public-benefit status in mind; please check
  them against the current requirements for such organisations.
- **§ 16** gives communities a voice without making them the Foundation's members (a foundation has none). Whether
  a legal person may name a council member this way, and how to word it, is a question for you.
- **§ 22** is meant to make the name and the hub impossible to privatise.
